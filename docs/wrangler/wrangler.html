Hope Turtle Project

Turtle Wrangler

An Android app that puts the turtle's cockpit in your pocket — see what it sees, and steer it from your phone, without ever touching the tiny screen on the hull.

Proposal — not yet built

The problem today

Right now, everything about checking on or adjusting a Hope Turtle happens through a single small screen and a single button on the hull.

Today

  • A 1.3-inch OLED screen, one action button for control
  • Different numbers of clicks open different menus — single, double, triple, quadruple, or a long hold — genuinely tedious in practice
  • The OLED washes out and can't be read in direct daylight
  • Checking battery, sail, GPS and heading means clicking through several separate screens, one at a time
  • Setting a destination or starting a journey is a multi-step button sequence you have to remember
  • Any control at all is impossible unless you're physically right beside the turtle, in the water
  • If a stamp or a command fails, the tiny screen is easy to miss — or it gives no reason at all

With Turtle Wrangler

  • A full phone screen, easy to read even outdoors
  • Tap what you want — no click-counting to memorize
  • Works from up to 5 metres away — no wading in beside the hull
  • Battery, sail, heading, and position all visible together, at a glance
  • Setting a destination or starting a journey is a couple of taps
  • Manual GPS stamps are one big button — and the turtle tells you straight away if one didn't take
  • Every command gets an answer: done, or exactly why not

What the app actually does

Four simple tabs plus a settings page, each replacing a part of what the on-hull screen does today — just easier to read and easier to use. Every button in the app does something the turtle's own button can already do, so nothing is lost when a phone isn't around.

📊

Dashboard

One glance tells you heading, battery level, sail angle, GPS position, and how far the turtle still has to travel — live, the moment your phone is near it.

🧭

Navigate

Set where the turtle should head, start or end a test run, and see how far off-course it currently is — all with taps instead of button-click codes.

📍

GPS stamps

Switch the turtle to manual logging and record its position with one big tap — each stamp is confirmed, counted, and tracked until it reaches the website.

🔧

Diagnostics

Run a quick sail test to find the wind, and check at a glance that every sensor on board — compass, battery monitor, GPS — is healthy before launch.

⚙️

Settings

Hand the turtle its WiFi details and adjust how often it reports home — no laptop or cable required in the field.

🔒

Safe for real voyages

Pairing a phone needs a 6-digit code shown on the turtle itself, so only someone standing beside it can take control. Before a real mission, Bluetooth can be switched off completely.

How it would work, in practice

1

Walk up with your phone

Open the app anywhere within about 5 metres of the turtle — no WiFi network or internet connection needed.

2

It finds the turtle

The app finds the turtle over Bluetooth (the same short-range wireless a pair of headphones uses). The first time, you pair by typing the 6-digit code the turtle shows on its own screen.

3

See it, tap it, done

Live readings appear immediately. Any change you make — a new destination, a GPS stamp, a sail test — reaches the turtle in under a second, and the turtle answers back: done, or exactly why not.

Why this is worth doing

Faster field checks. A full status readout in seconds instead of cycling through several tiny screens.

Wayyyy less tedious. No more misremembering whether it's a double-click or triple-click to reach a setting.

Easier to train new team members. A phone app is familiar; a button-code sequence on a 1-inch screen is not.

Better diagnostics before launch. One screen confirms every sensor is healthy before a turtle goes in the water.

No extra hardware. Any Android phone works — nothing new to buy or carry.

No silent failures. Every tap gets a clear answer from the turtle — "no GPS fix yet", "clock not set" — instead of a screen that just doesn't change.

Locked down at sea. Bluetooth only opens for a few minutes at a time, pairing needs the code on the hull, and it can be switched off entirely for a real mission.

Doesn't replace the shore tracking system. This is for hands-on fieldwork; the existing website tracking stays exactly as is.

What building this involves

Two pieces of work now, one on each side of the Bluetooth connection — and a third, smaller one planned for later.

Turtle side

A small firmware update

The turtle's onboard computer needs to learn how to broadcast its data over Bluetooth and accept a small set of commands — an addition to the existing system, not a rebuild of it.

Phone side

A new Android app

Built from scratch, but a well-understood kind of app — the screens sketched above, talking to the turtle over Bluetooth.

Website side · later

Turtles at sea

A small, read-only addition to hopeturtles.org so a logged-in Buwana user can see their own turtles from the app when they're out of Bluetooth range but reporting over WiFi.

Version 2.0

Down the road...

Once the first version is out and proven in the field, here's the vision for where Turtle Wrangler could go next. None of this is committed work — it's the shape of a second phase, once the core app has earned its keep.

🌊

Turtles at sea — next up

Log in with your Buwana account and follow the turtles you look after while they're far out of Bluetooth range — position, battery and track, straight from the website. View-only: steering still happens beside the turtle.

🍏

iOS version

The same app, rebuilt for iPhone, so the team isn't limited to Android hardware in the field.

🐧

Ubuntu Touch version

A build for UBports' open-source mobile OS, for teams who prefer to stay off the mainstream app stores entirely.

🗺️

Offline map tile caching

Maps that keep working with no signal at all — useful for genuinely remote coastlines where the phone has no connection either.

🐢

Multi-turtle fleet view

One screen showing every turtle nearby over Bluetooth at once, for the day a launch involves more than one hull at a time.

🏬

Android store listing

A public, polished listing on the Play Store, so the app is a normal download rather than a file passed hand to hand.
